Building a cost diagnostic, and arguing against it first.

DriftLine started as an idea: let a company upload its P&L and get told where to save money. Before building it properly, I reviewed the idea the way a strategy partner would, sized the market for Indian MSMEs, decided to make it free, and set a roadmap with clear points to stop.

Partner review · product concept

Is a cost-cutting tool built on annual statements a business?

A deliberately skeptical read of the concept, written the way a steering committee would hear it. The live diagnostic exists to test these points, not to argue past them.

Verdict

The concept as stated has a structural flaw: a P&L and balance sheet can tell you where to look, but almost never what to do. "Other expenses rose from 2.2% to 2.6% of revenue" is a real signal; "cut travel by renegotiating your top three vendors" requires vendor-level spend data that annual statements do not contain. If you promise the second and deliver the first, CFOs will churn after the first report because they already know their ratios.

The idea becomes viable if you reposition it: annual statements are the free entry diagnostic (fast, no integration, good for lead generation), and the paid product connects to the accounting system for ledger- and vendor-level data, adds peer benchmarks, and tracks whether savings are actually realised. The rest of this page explains why.

Questions I would ask you

Customer and value

  1. Who exactly is the buyer? A CFO of a ₹500 Cr company has a finance team that already computes these ratios. An MSME owner lacks the analysis but has low willingness to pay. These are different products.
  2. What decision does the user make the day after reading the report? If you can't name one, the report is interesting, not valuable.
  3. Why would they not upload the same statements into a general AI assistant? That is now free and good enough for ratio commentary. Your moat cannot be the commentary.
  4. How will you prove savings were realised? Without measurement you are selling opinions, and you cannot price on outcomes.
Questions I would ask you

Data and method

  1. What is your benchmark source? "Your power cost is high" needs a peer set by industry and size. In India that means MCA filings or licensed databases (e.g. CMIE Prowess). Building that is a real moat; skipping it leaves only self-comparison.
  2. How will you separate inefficiency from price and mix? A material cost ratio rising from 60% to 62.5% might be commodity inflation the company could not control, a shift to a lower-margin product, or genuine waste. Statements alone cannot tell these apart.
  3. How will you get clean data in? Real statements arrive as scanned PDFs with notes, regroupings and restatements. Extraction and line-item mapping is most of the engineering effort, not the analytics.
  4. Three to five annual data points is a small sample. How do you avoid presenting noise or one-off items as trends?

Facts, assumptions and judgments in the concept

TypeStatement
FactAnnual statements show cost by nature (materials, employee, power) at an aggregated level. They do not show vendors, contracts, units consumed or prices paid.
FactCost ratios and working-capital days can be computed reliably from them, and changes over time are visible.
AssumptionThat companies lack this analysis today. Likely true for small firms, likely false for mid-size firms with a finance team or a retained CA.
AssumptionThat a company will act on a software-generated recommendation without a person to own it. Cost programmes usually fail on execution, not identification.
AssumptionThat companies will upload confidential financials to a new vendor. Listed companies' statements are public anyway; private companies will ask hard security questions.
InferenceBecause the input is coarse, specific "do X" recommendations from statements alone will often be generic. Quality rises sharply once ledger data is available.
JudgmentThe defensible business is benchmarks + ledger integration + savings tracking, with statement upload as the entry point.
Steelman

The strongest case for building it as you described

Most small and mid-size companies never get a structured outside look at their cost base. A ten-minute, low-friction diagnostic that says "these four lines drifted, this is what it is worth, here are the questions to ask" is more than many of them get today. It needs no integration, works from documents they already file, and can be sold through chartered-accountant firms who serve thousands of clients. Being directionally right on where to look is useful even if the "how" stays with the client.

What you are missing

Risks and blind spots

  • Cutting the wrong cost. Marketing, R&D and maintenance cuts improve this year's margin and damage next year's revenue or asset life. The tool must say when not to cut.
  • Revenue is usually the bigger lever. A one-point price increase often beats a year of cost work. A pure cost tool ignores pricing and mix.
  • Liability. A wrong recommendation that leads to layoffs or supplier loss is a reputational risk. Frame output as diagnosis, not advice.
  • Existing players. FP&A tools (for example Fathom, Spotlight Reporting, Datarails, Cube), accounting suites with analytics, and CA firms already sit in this workflow. Distribution may matter more than features.

How the product responds to these critiques

  • It sizes savings against the company's own best year, not an invented benchmark, and lets you set the capture rate instead of assuming 100%.
  • Every insight separates what the numbers show from what they cannot show, and lists the data to pull next.
  • Working capital is reported as cash release, with only the interest saving counted as P&L impact.
  • Depreciation, finance cost and tax are excluded from "controllable" savings.
Market sizing · Indian MSMEs

The real audience is much smaller than "MSMEs in India".

India has crores of registered MSMEs, but most are informal micro units with no balance sheet to analyse. The product needs several years of clean statements, and a cost gap large enough in rupees to be worth paying to find. That narrows the audience to about 3.4 lakh firms.

Why I made it free

  • Adoption is the hard part. MSME owners rarely pay for software beyond Tally and their CA. A price would filter out the people the tool is for.
  • It costs almost nothing to run. The analysis happens in the visitor's browser. Only Plop's answers use a paid AI service, and those have a fair-use limit.
  • Usage is the evidence I need. Whether owners run it, upload real statements and come back says more than early sign-ups for a paid plan would.

If it ever needs to earn money

  • CA firms: about 99,000 in India. A multi-client edition could be sold to the firms rather than charged to MSMEs.
  • Lenders: banks and NBFCs analyse MSME statements for every loan. Fewer buyers, but each pays far more.
  • The test first: ask owners and CAs what they pay today for anything beyond Tally and audit fees.

Roadmap · 18 months

Validate first, then earn the right to deeper data

Each phase has a test it must pass before the next one is funded. The order matters: building ledger integrations before you know which insights drive action is the most likely way to waste the first year.

Weeks 0–6

Phase 0 · Validate the problem

Build

  • This prototype, plus a manual "concierge" diagnostic run by a person for 10–15 companies
  • 15–20 interviews: CFOs, MSME owners, CA partners

Learn

  • Which buyer feels the pain most and will pay
  • Which insights led to an actual action within 30 days
  • Whether CA firms want it as a client-service tool

Pass / kill

  • Pass: ≥ 5 of 15 companies act on a finding and ≥ 3 would recommend it to a peer or their CA
  • Kill or pivot: reports are called "interesting" but nobody acts
Months 2–4

Phase 1 · Statement diagnostic MVP

Build

  • PDF and Excel extraction for Schedule III and common formats, with a human review step
  • Line-item mapping, ratio engine, own-history sizing, chat grounded in the data
  • Security basics: encryption, deletion on request, no training on client data

Learn

  • Extraction accuracy on real statements
  • Time from upload to report
  • Share of visitors who upload their own statements

Metrics

  • ≥ 95% line-item mapping accuracy after review
  • Report in under 10 minutes
  • ≥ 10% of visitors sign up for updates
Months 4–9

Phase 2 · Benchmarks and ledger data

Build

  • Peer benchmark set by industry and revenue band (MCA filings, licensed databases)
  • Connectors to accounting systems common with your buyers (Tally, Zoho Books, SAP B1)
  • Vendor spend cube: spend by vendor, category and month

Learn

  • Whether peer gaps change recommendations versus own-history gaps
  • Which spend categories have the largest addressable gap

Metrics

  • Recommendations name a vendor or contract, not just a line item
  • Pilots with ≥ 10 companies using ledger data
Months 9–18

Phase 3 · From insight to realised savings

Build

  • Initiative tracker: owner, target, due date per saving
  • Monthly realised-savings measurement against baseline, adjusted for volume and price
  • Lever library by category; pricing and mix analysis on the revenue side
  • White-label edition for CA and advisory firms

Learn

  • Identified-to-realised savings conversion
  • Whether CA firms or lenders would fund a free product for MSMEs

Metrics

  • ≥ 40% of identified savings realised within 12 months
  • ≥ 40% of companies come back within 6 months
Beyond 18 months

Phase 4 · Planning layer

Build

  • Rolling forecast and scenario planning (commodity, wage, FX shocks)
  • Alerts when a cost line drifts month on month

Why later

  • Planning is a crowded FP&A market. Enter it only with proprietary benchmark and savings data competitors lack.

Risk

  • Feature sprawl that dilutes the cost wedge before it has proven retention

Most likely way this fails

The team spends nine months perfecting PDF extraction and charts, ships a polished report, and discovers that buyers already knew their ratios and wanted someone to negotiate with suppliers. Phase 0 exists to find that out in six weeks instead.
